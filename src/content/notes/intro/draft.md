# what are these notes?

While i love doing reseach, and am lucky enough to be paid to do so, it can be quite restrictive in its form. From introduction to related work to methods etc. provides the structure that is useful and efficient for the reader of your paper, but is a bit stale for the writer of it. 

Same thing goes for the research subject. My research has is constrained to (hopefully) scientifically relevant topics, that help push our domain (and the world) forward. Everything has to be peer-reviewed to check the novelty, vailidity and the soundness of the work, and for good reason obviously.  

These notes are essentially my small personal research projects. I don't have to care about preregistrations or p-values (or other scientific p-starting words), and can decide for myself when the work is done. Whenever I have a question not directly answerable by google, or when I've read something interesting that I have an opinion on, I want to write it down here. 
## why write them?

One of my earliest memories using a computer as a child were of me writing out stories in Word. I used to love writing tales on paper, but having the power of the home computer (pronounced kontjoeter  back then) in the living room opened up a world of opportunities. Being able to go online and look for images to match the story has been ingrained into my memories, as it felt like the future was here. 

Fast forward to now, and the future is even more here than I could have thought of then. The kontjoeter has become the central machinery in my (and most peoples) life, and have more capabilities than just googling fun images for storytelling sake. Especially in my work, where AI is not only the main research subject but also more and more the research tool (or even a competitor), I sometimes miss the feeling of creating something myself. While these tools can help in areas your less familiar with, they should not become replacements for your own skills.

I love these tools, and find it fascinating to see how the world has changed ever since I started studying AI in 2015. However, I want to use them as supporting tools to feed my curiousity instead of replacing it. These notes are mostly here for me to practice freewriting and to have a place where I can dump my micro obsession with questions that pop up in my brain. The less interesting parts (implemention of the website, cleaning data, checking spelling) are the parts I outsource to the artificially intelligent. 

## Why now?

An (old colleague)[joris veerbeek at de groene] back the the Data School in Utrecht used to combine his PhD with his work as a journalist at De Groene[link]. This has always been inspiring for me, as he could use his findings and tools that he acquired from his 'formal' research and write about them in an unconstrained manner in his articles. I love reading his work and still follow every article he writes. 

Similarily, some of the articles from the NYT and NRC, especially those curated for interactive web interfaces are sources of inspiration, [election examples for both] although more so in their clean way presenting information and writing quality than in terms of content. 

The biggest source of inspiration is probably pudding [;ink] though. Their work is so diverse and so uniquely presented, while still being consistently interesting. If you have time, look into [three examples] .
