# How diverse is my spotify

Spotify wrapped is the gift everyone's favorite chrsitmas present. Seeing your own horrible taste, or silently judging others. Although i look forward to it every year, it does not really fulfill my desires. I want to know more. Just having a year of music does not give you insight into your taste development, nor does it tell you anything beyond your favorite song of the year. 

Part of my hunger for more information was already stilled by downloading stats.fm (which was still called Spotistats back then) a few years ago. Its one of the few apps I have even paid for with all the google rewards I had gathered over the years. This tool allowed me to not just look at last years stats, but showed me my favorite artists (Future), most played albums (Chopin's Nocturnes) and my top song of all time (Time Moves Slow). These can all be seen already on my music page, including more recent songs I listen to. 

However, I wanted more. I had a feeling my music taste had evolved throughout the years, and had become more diverse. But looking at my top songs and artists of all time did not really give me any insight into that. Although this was slightly more dynamic, it ends up being a static blob of information as new songs or artists have a lot of catching up to do in order to penetrate my top 10's. So here is my "deep dive" into the last decade of my music listening history, starting in 2015. 

## fighting the algorithm
Fresh out of highschool, ready to start studying. I remember using Spotify completely different than I do now. I used to create playlist for every genre, mood or niche I was interested in. Either listening to just full albums, or to these special playlist. Nowadays I feel like I let "the algorithm" take the wheel, and rely much more on dynamic playlists, or on songradio's. Albums are still my favorite to listen to though, as you can crawl into the head of an artist much more easily, and see (or hear) their ideas much clearer. That being said, my first album I added was Flockaveli by Waka Flocka Flame, so I'm not sure if this is the vision I was going for. 

<data on listening diversity: playlist vs. albums. vs likes etc. >

Now unfortunately, the data of where you listened from only start in 2024, meaning my handcrafted playlist era cannot be seen. However, I can still see how the algorithm has taking over, although it is more close to lane assistnt instead of full automated driving. Full albums still dominate my listening habits, but in my playlists curation has been outsourced to the computer. Not knowing what to listen to in the morning and just opening my daylist or one of the daily playlists seems to be a go to source. The only exception seems to be the first playlist I ever made: Tunes. This was essentially my likes back in the day, and only consisted of hiphop. 

<decline of tunes: can just be a line chart or something simple>

Reverse engineering my listening history and matching which songs were in Tunes, this becomes more clearly visible. It used to be essentially all I listened to, and has now become more of a trip down memory lane with some sporadic revisits. I started using likes around 2022, and ever since then this has been primary  way of listinging to diverse music. 

## genre dominance
Seeing Tunes still so high up the charts after 2024, I wanted to see what my genre shift over the years has been like. Although I would guess (or essentially know) hiphop is still very prevelant in my listening habits, but I feel like I've also diversified in terms of genres a lot more

<genre evolution: the distribution chart maybe Streamgraph if I can understand the thickness.  >

Seems like my gut feeling was right. From over 60% at the start of my listening history, to only a quarter of my plays now. This gap seems to have been filled by more indie music, which I used to listen to a lot more before joining Spotify (my CD collection is essentially only indie and rock). I'm not quite sure what caused this change, but it is not just nostalgia. New artists in this genre (yussuf dayes [is this not just jazz, whatever], men I trust, and the strokes) are side by side with older stuff (vampire weekend, arctic monkeys, and bloc party). 

## the patriarchy
Looking at my indie artists I was quite suprised to not see many women pop up. I know that I don't listen to female hiphop (little simz probably as the only exception?), but I'd expected to see at least some of my girlies pop up. How patriachrical is my listening history even?

<gender divide of listening history>

It's almost embarrising to see that in the first years not even a tenth of the songs were performed by a women. Looking back this is not too suprising given what i was listening to, but seeing the statistics it was still a bit of a shock. glad to see that I turned that around and am now at least a bit more diverse. we can defeat the patriarchy one song at a time guys! 

## my faves
my favorites artists of all time are not following the same trend though. looking at the overall lists, the first women (excluding the pianist of the chopin album at #19) is the lead vocalist of crumb at #38, and faye webster at #41. the top artists are unsuprising, both because I know I have listened (and still listen) to these artists a lot, but also because this is one of the stats I signed up for stats.fm in the first place.

<top artists of all time>

one thing i could not see was how long these artists stayed with me throughout the years. I know future keeps popping up in spotify wrapped each year, but many artists also seem to come and go. how many actually reappear and who just simply fades away to never be listened to again.

only a handful of artists stay in my top 10 throughout the years. It looks like some artists come and go, while my household names stay pretty consistent. diversity and consistency at the same time, a nice balance. 

## where am i

## i love data

my biggest conclusion from this undertaking is I love data. being able to really look into my own listening behaviour like this is just fascinating, especially since I have almost a decade of it. lets see how things shift in the following decade
