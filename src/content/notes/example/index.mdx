A note starts with a question in plain words. Say: how many artists do I actually listen to? The opening paragraph should make the reader want the answer before they see a single chart.

This is ordinary Markdown: **bold**, *italic*, [links](https://pudding.cool), lists and headings all work. The components below are imported at the top of the file.

## One chart, one sentence

The main claim gets a figure. Its title *is* the finding, and the colour points at it: <Mark>ten artists make up almost half of everything</Mark>, and they're the only thing in terracotta.

<Figure
  title="Ten artists make up almost half of my plays"
  subtitle="Cumulative share of plays, artists ranked from most to least played (log scale)"
  source="Made-up example data"
>
  <Chart name="concentration" data={artists} height={300} alt="A curve rising steeply: the top ten artists account for about 45% of plays." />
</Figure>

<Aside>
Margin notes go here: a tangent, a caveat about the data, a link that didn't fit. Put the Aside right before the paragraph it belongs to.
</Aside>

Text continues after the figure. Hover over the chart for exact numbers: that's Plot's built-in tooltip, one line in `charts.ts`. Interactivity should answer a question a reader would naturally have ("what's the number at this point?"), not just decorate.

Figures span both columns by default. Use `width="text"` for a small chart that should stay in the reading column.

## A scrolly section

For the one showpiece of a note, the chart can stay put while the text scrolls past it. Each step tells the chart what to highlight.

<Scrolly>
  <Chart slot="graphic" name="yearly" data={years} height={360} alt="Bars of distinct artists per year, 2015 to 2025, with a jump in 2020." />
  <Step>Here is every year since 2015: how many different artists I played at least once.</Step>
  <Step>2020 stands out. Something happened that year (spoiler: a lockdown and a lot of new playlists).</Step>
  <Step>And it never really went back. Every year since sits well above where things were before.</Step>
</Scrolly>

## Ending

End with what you'd tell a friend about it, plus a short note on method and sources. That's it: a question, a few charts, and a story connecting them.
