For our course on Human Centered Machine Learning, we assessed Sharma et al.'s
counterfactual-based fairness metric and presented it at the final poster
session. Our instructor liked the idea and asked whether we wanted to submit it
to a conference, which turned out to be a great way to see the academic world
beyond our studies.

<figure>
  <img src="/news/2022-06-30-burden-bubble-poster.jpg" alt="The four of us standing beside our poster at the course poster session" />
  <figcaption>The poster session that started it, Utrecht.</figcaption>
</figure>

We sent the work to the Benelux Conference on Artificial Intelligence and were
accepted as an oral presentation. It was great to spend time with good people
in Mechelen, and it planted the seed for thinking about a PhD afterwards. It
also spoiled me a little: they hired a magician for the conference dinner, and
no conference since has quite lived up to it.
